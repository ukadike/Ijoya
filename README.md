# Ìjóyà

**Movement · interdependence · accessible embodied systems**

Ìjóyà is a Small Systems Lab project exploring how bodies, materials, environments, and support systems become capable through relationship.

Its current major project is **_Ìjóyà: Borrowed Function in the Garden_**, also developed under the working title **_Borrowed Function: A Garden of Interdependence_**.

The project proposes six outdoor soft sculptural bodies that are incomplete or unstable alone and become capable through shared tension, wind, balance, support, flexible joints, cords, and articulated connections. Interdependence is treated as ordinary, intelligent, and generative rather than as heroic overcoming.

> This is not a collection of robots in a garden. It is one distributed body expressed through multiple incomplete bodies.

## Current Project

### Ìjóyà: Borrowed Function in the Garden

**Status:** proposed / concept and maquette development

**Medium:** outdoor soft sculpture and environmental observation

Potential materials and systems include:

- weather-resistant textile and mesh;
- soft sculptural volumes;
- flexible armatures;
- braided cord and mechanical connectors;
- tactile cowrie nodes;
- low-voltage light, vibration, and sensing;
- selective soft robotics where they materially support the sculptural relationship.

Technology is subordinate to the sculptural relationship. The project does **not** require AI or autonomous decision-making.

The garden is not a backdrop. Wind, touch, paths, plant-support structures, visitor movement, and changing environmental conditions become part of the network of forces through which the work is encountered.

See [PROJECT.md](PROJECT.md) for the project brief and [ACCESSIBILITY.md](ACCESSIBILITY.md) for the access framework.

## Accessibility

Accessibility is part of the work's spatial and material design rather than an interpretive layer added afterward. Current design commitments include:

- distinct silhouettes;
- strong visual contrast;
- multiple viewing heights;
- accessible pathways and seated sightlines;
- large-print interpretation;
- audio description;
- tactile material samples;
- multimodal ways of understanding connection, tension, support, and response.

See [ACCESSIBILITY.md](ACCESSIBILITY.md).

## Relationship to Small Systems Lab

Ìjóyà is part of [Small Systems Lab](https://ukadike.github.io/Small-Systems-Lab/), a public-interest studio for tender systems.

Within that ecosystem:

- **Earth Sensors Lab** develops environmental sensing and accessible scientific observation that may inform future embodied or environmental experiments without turning Ìjóyà into a data visualization project.
- **Omoluabi** contributes methods for provenance, evidence, human review, and keeping different kinds of records distinct.
- **Accessible by Design** provides tooling and methods for auditing digital and multimodal interfaces associated with the work.
- **UMADA** explores speculative systems and future histories; any relationship remains clearly separated from Ìjóyà's physical installation record.

## Truth Boundary

Current drawings, studies, videos, and written descriptions are **concept and maquette studies**, not documentation of completed sculptures or a completed installation.

No residency selection, institutional partnership, fabrication, testing, or disabled-participant validation is claimed unless documented as completed.
