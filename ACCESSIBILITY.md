# Ìjóyà — Accessibility Architecture

Accessibility is part of the physical, spatial, sensory, and interpretive design of Ìjóyà from the beginning.

## Current Design Commitments

- Distinct silhouettes so relationships between sculptural bodies can be understood without relying only on color.
- Strong contrast where appropriate between forms, connectors, pathways, and surrounding vegetation.
- Multiple viewing heights and seated sightlines.
- Accessible pathways and circulation around the installation.
- Large-print interpretation.
- Audio description.
- Tactile material samples and explicit guidance about which installation elements may be touched.
- Multimodal descriptions of tension, support, movement, and connection.
- Technology that does not require fine-motor precision or visual-only interaction where visitor interaction is offered.

## Disability Framing

The project does not frame dependency as tragedy, defect, or heroic overcoming. Interdependence is treated as ordinary infrastructure: bodies and systems become capable through support, adaptation, redistribution, and relation.

The sculptures are not stand-ins for disabled people. Accessibility and disability experience inform the project's ethics, spatial design, and understanding of support without reducing disability to metaphor.

## Environmental Access

Outdoor installation introduces changing access conditions: weather, ground surface, glare, sound, crowding, seasonal vegetation, and changing light. These must be considered as part of the installation rather than treated as external problems.

## Validation Status

These are design commitments, not a claim of completed accessibility validation. Final access decisions require site review, prototype testing, assistive-technology checks where digital media is involved, and disabled-participant review where feasible.
