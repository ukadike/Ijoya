## The proposal as submitted

**Title:** Ìjóyà: Borrowed Function in the Garden

**Dimensions:** Site-responsive installation of approximately six human-scale to larger-than-human-scale bodies; final dimensions and spacing to be determined through Winter Workspace site study.

**Media:** Weather-resistant textile/mesh, soft sculptural volumes, flexible armatures, braided cord, mechanical connectors, tactile cowrie nodes, low-voltage light/vibration/sensing components and selective soft robotics.

Ìjóyà is a group of incomplete bodies that cannot fully function alone. One may sense but depend on another to respond. One may need tension from a neighboring body to remain upright. Movement, pressure, vibration, touch and environmental signals can travel across the group.

The garden is part of that network. Wind may move one body and alter another. A visitor's touch may produce a response elsewhere. Cowrie forms operate as cultural and functional interfaces — eyes, tactile landmarks, signals and connection points — not decoration.

This is not a collection of robots in a garden. It is one distributed body expressed through multiple incomplete bodies. Their collective movement is the dance: we dance in the garden together because we cannot function alone.

## Why this site

A garden already understands interdependence. Plants, soil, weather, walls, supports, gardeners, visitors and paths are constantly affecting one another. The aim is to learn from that system and make a sculpture that does not just represent interdependence, but actually has to practice it.

## Winter Workspace plan

**Period:** February 23 – April 18, 2027

The Workspace is for figuring out what these bodies actually need from one another.

I do not want to arrive with a finished sculpture and simply place it in the garden. I want the garden to change the work.

**Site study** — using observation methods developed through Earth Sensors Lab: wind, light, moisture, temperature, plant movement, existing supports, pathways, accessible routes, seated sightlines, and how people move through the site.

**Prototyping at increasing scale** — textile skins, flexible armatures, braided connections, cowrie-shaped tactile and signal nodes, pressure, vibration, tension, light, and selective soft-robotic movement.

The questions being tested:

- What happens when one body senses something but another body responds?
- What happens when wind moves one form and that movement changes the posture of another?
- What happens when touch in one place can be felt somewhere else?

The drawings that exist now are concept and maquette studies. **The Workspace is where they become bodies.** By the end of the period the material, structural and communication system should be resolved enough that the final human-scale and larger-than-human-scale installation can inhabit the garden as one distributed body.

## Site constraints to design against

- Wave Hill is a family-friendly public garden. No nudity, depictions of violence, or profanity.
- Outdoor installation across a full spring season — weather exposure, temperature swing, moisture.
- Public, unsupervised visitor contact. Anything touchable has to survive being touched repeatedly by people of all ages, and has to fail safely.
- Accessible routes and seated sightlines are part of the composition, not an afterthought.
