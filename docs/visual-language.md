# Visual language

The visual language is a constraint, not a mood board. Its purpose is to keep the work legible as sculpture about relationship rather than as a technology demonstration.

## Principles

**Handmade rather than slick.** Visible seams, stitching, cords, joints and supports. The construction should be readable — a visitor should be able to see how a body is held together and how it is held up by something else.

**Textile rather than generic robotics.** Soft volumes, fabric and mesh skins, braided connections. Rigid machined parts appear only where structurally necessary and stay subordinate.

**Warm, earthy, materially legible.** Earth and fiber tones. Materials that read as material.

**Difference is visible.** Bodies differ from one another in silhouette, capacity, posture and support. Visibly different bodies and capacities carry the meaning — not icons, not labels.

**African material and cultural vocabulary integrated structurally.** Cowrie forms function as interface. Pattern and surface belong to construction rather than being applied as ornament.

**Technology partially exposed but subordinate.** Enough visibility that a visitor can understand something is happening and roughly how. Never enough that the electronics become the subject.

**Diagrammatic when explanation helps.** Annotation, arrows and labels are legitimate in study material, where the job is to communicate system logic.

**Encountered at scale.** Human scale to larger-than-human scale, inhabiting the garden spatially and bodily.

## Avoid

- Generic glossy robotics
- Toy-robot framing
- Colorful network graphics as the dominant aesthetic
- Futuristic UI graphics becoming the artwork
- Accessibility reduced to icons
- Imagery implying the installation has already been fabricated

## On scale and the preliminary drawings

The existing studies show small, doll-like bodies. This is a limitation of the study material, not the intended form.

At scale the bodies should evolve away from the visual language of dolls or toy robots — becoming softer, more abstract, more sculptural, more botanical, more materially expressive, more integrated with the landscape, and less conventionally humanoid.

**The concept is preserved by the relationships among bodies, not by reproducing the exact proportions of the preliminary drawings.**

This matters when generating or commissioning new visual material. A new study that faithfully reproduces the doll proportions is reproducing the wrong thing.

## Known weaknesses in current material

Honest assessment of what exists, so it can be fixed rather than repeated:

- Only five usable studies, three of which are genuinely strong.
- No study shows the bodies at human scale.
- No study shows the bodies in a garden context resembling the proposed site.
- Two of the five are text-and-diagram slides rather than images of bodies.
- Earlier material included toy-robot imagery and unrelated project renders that contradicted this document; those have been excluded.

The highest-value visual work is a study showing human-scale bodies in garden context, in the material vocabulary above.
