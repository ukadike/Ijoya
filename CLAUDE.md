# CLAUDE.md — Working instructions for Ìjóyà

Read `docs/project-logic.md` before doing anything in this repo. It is canonical. Where any other file disagrees with it, project-logic.md wins.

## What this project is

Ìjóyà is one distributed body expressed through multiple incomplete bodies. The bodies borrow balance, sensing, movement, communication, tension and support from one another. Their collective movement is the dance.

**The relationship is the artwork.** The bodies make that relationship visible, tactile, spatial and alive.

## Non-negotiables

1. **Preserve the title spelling exactly:** `Ìjóyà: Borrowed Function in the Garden`. Diacritics on both the Ì and the ó/à. Never write "Ijoya" in prose, headings, or metadata. Slugs and file paths may use `ijoya`.
2. **Do not add AI or LLM functionality to the artwork.** Selective sensing, haptics, light, mechanics and soft robotics are sufficient. The project explicitly does not require autonomous decision-making. This is a stated artistic position, not an unexplored option.
3. **Technology stays subordinate to the sculptural relationship.** A sensor, motor, microcontroller, light or actuator exists only where it makes interdependence physically perceptible.
4. **Never describe study material as a completed or fabricated work.** Everything in `assets/` and `studies/` is concept and maquette study material. Nothing has been built at scale. Captions must say so.
5. **Accessibility is communication architecture, not accommodation.** Do not reduce it to icons, a compliance checklist, or a section appended at the end.
6. **Cowrie forms are functional and cultural interface elements** — tactile landmarks, signal nodes, sensing metaphors, illuminated interfaces, connection points. Never decoration, never appliqué.
7. **The garden is a participant, not a backdrop.**
8. **Earth Sensors Lab is a distinct project.** It supplies the environmental-observation methodology. Do not merge the two or let ESL material become the visual identity of Ìjóyà.
9. **Keep categories accurate.** Fellowship ≠ residency ≠ teaching ≠ employment ≠ education. Eyebeam was employment. Lincoln Center and Processing Foundation were fellowships. There have been no prior artist residencies.

## Voice

Preserve the artist's direct first-person language. Do not make prose more institutional for the sake of polish, and do not substitute generic grant-writing phrasing.

Locked phrases — reproduce verbatim, do not paraphrase:

- "We dance in the garden together because we cannot function alone."
- "needing support is not the opposite of functioning"
- "We function because we are connected."
- "I do not want to make a medicalized work about disability or a story of heroic overcoming."
- "I want to make something alive, strange, tender and joyful."
- "I do not want to arrive with a finished sculpture and simply place it in the garden. I want the garden to change the work."
- "The Workspace is where they become bodies."
- "This is not a collection of robots in a garden."
- "The relationship is the artwork."

## Visual language

**Favor:** stitched, textile, tactile, handmade bodies · warm earth and fiber tones · visible seams, cords, joints, supports and connections · cowrie interface forms · visibly different bodies and capacities · diagrammatic annotation where useful · technology visible enough to understand but never dominant · human-scale and larger-than-human-scale garden inhabitation.

**Avoid:** generic glossy robotics · toy-robot framing · colorful network graphics as the dominant aesthetic · futuristic UI graphics becoming the artwork · accessibility reduced to icons · anything implying the installation has been fabricated.

## The design test

Apply to every proposed addition, in code, documentation or fabrication:

> Does this make interdependence physically perceptible, or is it merely adding technology or decoration?

If it does not strengthen the relationship among bodies, garden and visitors, reconsider it.

## Working conventions

- Documentation is Markdown. Keep it readable in plain text — this repo is read by people using screen readers.
- Every image needs alt text. Every video needs a caption and a text description. This is a project about access; the repo has to practice it.
- Where a claim is aspirational rather than tested, say so. Do not let planned systems read as built systems.
- Prefer editing existing docs over creating parallel ones. If a new doc is genuinely needed, link it from `README.md`.
- Do not invent exhibition history, press, funding, collaborators, or technical results.

## What needs work

Current gaps, in rough priority order:

1. Physical prototyping has not started. No material, structural or connection system has been tested.
2. Visual study material is thin — five usable studies. More Ìjóyà-specific studies at human scale, in garden context, would strengthen the project's case everywhere it is presented.
3. The connection mechanism between bodies is specified conceptually but not resolved mechanically.
4. Weatherproofing and outdoor power strategy are unaddressed.
