Ìjóyà

Movement, interdependence, and accessible embodied systems.

Current project

Borrowed Function in the Garden

Six outdoor soft sculptural bodies are conceived as incomplete or unstable alone. They become capable through shared tension, wind, balance, support, flexible joints, cords, and articulated connections.

This is not a collection of robots in a garden. It is one distributed body expressed through multiple incomplete bodies.

Material relationship

Weather-resistant textile and mesh, soft volumes, flexible armatures, braided cord, mechanical connectors, tactile cowrie nodes, and selective low-voltage light, vibration, sensing, or soft robotics.

The garden participates

Wind, touch, paths, plant-support structures, visitor movement, changing light, and seasonal conditions become part of how relationships between the bodies are encountered.

Accessibility

Distinct silhouettes, strong contrast, multiple viewing heights, accessible paths and seated sightlines, large-print interpretation, audio description, and tactile material samples.

Truth boundary

Current drawings, videos, and written studies are concept and maquette work. They are not presented as documentation of completed sculptures or a completed installation.

Project position

Interdependence as infrastructure

The work does not treat dependency as tragedy, defect, or heroic overcoming. It asks how bodies and systems become capable through support, adaptation, redistribution, and relation.

Technology remains subordinate to sculptural relationship. The project does not require AI or autonomous decision-making.

Small Systems Lab ecosystem

Ìjóyà is one branch of a larger public-interest systems practice. Earth Sensors Lab develops environmental observation; Omoluabi develops evidence and provenance systems; Accessible by Design develops accessibility tooling; and UMADA is a separately governed speculative archive.
