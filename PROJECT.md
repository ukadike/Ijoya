# Ìjóyà: Borrowed Function in the Garden

**Status:** proposed / concept and maquette development  
**Project family:** Ìjóyà · Small Systems Lab  
**Medium:** outdoor soft sculpture and environmental observation

## Project

_Ìjóyà: Borrowed Function in the Garden_ proposes six soft sculptural bodies installed as a distributed outdoor system. Each body is intentionally incomplete or unstable alone. Capability emerges through shared tension, wind, balance, support, flexible joints, cords, articulated connections, and the presence of other bodies.

The project asks what changes when dependence is treated not as failure but as infrastructure.

Interdependence is therefore not represented symbolically after the fact. It is built into how the sculptures stand, flex, respond, lean, support, and redistribute force.

> This is not a collection of robots in a garden. It is one distributed body expressed through multiple incomplete bodies.

## Garden Relationship

The garden is an active system in the work.

Wind, touch, visitor movement, paths, planting, changing light, weather, plant-support structures, and seasonal conditions may participate in how the sculptures are encountered and how their relationships become legible.

The installation should remain site-responsive without claiming that every environmental change directly controls the work.

## Material Language

Current material studies may include:

- weather-resistant textile and mesh;
- soft volumes and padded or tensioned forms;
- flexible armatures;
- braided cord;
- mechanical connectors;
- tactile cowrie nodes;
- low-voltage light and vibration;
- sensing where it supports an observable material relationship;
- selective soft robotics where appropriate.

Technology remains subordinate to sculptural relationship. The project does not require AI or autonomous decision-making.

## Research Questions

- What does a body become capable of when support is distributed rather than hidden?
- How can tension, balance, flexibility, and dependency become visible physical relationships?
- How can outdoor sculpture communicate interdependence without turning disability into metaphor or heroic overcoming?
- How does a garden change the reading of support, vulnerability, adaptation, and shared infrastructure?
- How can visitors encounter the same sculptural relationships visually, spatially, tactually, and through audio description?

## Visitor Encounter

The work is designed to be encountered from multiple positions and heights. Visitors should be able to perceive distinct silhouettes and relationships between bodies without needing to stand in one privileged location.

Interpretive access may include large-print text, audio description, tactile samples, and clear descriptions of which elements are safe and intended to be touched.

## Evidence Boundary

Current drawings, videos, and written studies are concept and maquette material. They are not presented as documentation of a completed installation.

Fabrication choices, weather behavior, structural performance, sensing behavior, and accessibility validation remain subject to prototyping and documented testing.
