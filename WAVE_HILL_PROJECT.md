# Ìjóyà: Borrowed Function in the Garden

## Residency-facing project summary

_Ìjóyà: Borrowed Function in the Garden_ is an outdoor soft-sculpture project about interdependence as a material and spatial condition.

Six sculptural bodies are conceived as incomplete or unstable alone. They become capable through shared tension, wind, balance, support, flexible joints, cords, and articulated connections. The work treats interdependence as ordinary, intelligent, and generative rather than as heroic overcoming.

The garden participates in the installation. Wind, touch, visitor movement, paths, plant-support structures, changing light, and seasonal conditions become part of how relationships between the bodies are perceived.

Potential materials include weather-resistant textile and mesh, soft volumes, flexible armatures, braided cord, mechanical connectors, tactile cowrie nodes, and selective low-voltage light, vibration, sensing, or soft robotics. Technology remains subordinate to the sculptural relationship; the project does not require AI or autonomous decision-making.

Accessibility is embedded in the physical and interpretive design through distinct silhouettes, strong contrast, multiple viewing heights, accessible pathways and seated sightlines, large-print interpretation, audio description, and tactile material samples.

The central proposition is simple:

> This is not a collection of robots in a garden. It is one distributed body expressed through multiple incomplete bodies.

## Current status

The project is in concept and maquette development. Drawings, videos, and studies are not presented as documentation of completed sculptures. Fabrication, environmental behavior, structural performance, sensing, and accessibility decisions remain subject to prototype testing and site development.
